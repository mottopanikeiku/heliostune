2 · Selection and scoring are separate banks

Bank 1 timed every applicable action: all 36 original configurations, 6 new ordinary/persistent actions, and 4 split-K actions when M ≤ 31 (4,224 candidate cells). It selected the lowest valid median separately for the old set, the new set and their union, breaking ties by configuration key. Bank 2 then timed only those fixed finalists (192 candidate cells). Each bank also timed torch once per workload. No bank-2 minimum chooses an action. The action set and analysis plan were committed at adf5eb4 before collection (plan).

What bank 2 cannot identify. Bank 2 measures only the fixed old/new finalists and torch, not the entire action set. Full-bank oracle regret and per-family winner stability are unidentifiable. The common-action bank-pair view compares only these finalists. Among the two finalists only, the bank-1 union choice was also the faster finalist on bank 2 for 96/96 workloads; with a large old/new gap that agreement is easy, and it is not evidence of full-bank winner stability.

5 · Bank or selection? Every stored matrix

For each pair of banks in each stored archive, I pick every workload's fastest valid action on the earlier bank and score that fixed action on the later one, against the later bank's retrospective minimum (an optimistic in-sample oracle). In all 19 multi-bank views (several are overlapping copies of the same cells), the earlier-bank pick is at most 0.8% slower than that oracle (named geometric mean, per bank pair). The oracle itself ranges from 0.936× to 3.828× torch. My reading: on these measurements, choosing within a stored bank costs little; where the bank's best action is slower than torch, a better selector cannot close that gap.

Not policy replay. Exhaustive earlier-bank selection, then fixed-action later-bank scoring. This is a descriptive selector diagnosis, not a rerun of Thompson/transfer policies or a probe-budget result. Combined archives, raw/canonical validation and component acquisitions repeat identical cells (counted in shared_cells). They are alternative views, never independent replicates; do not sum rows or pool their statistics.

6 · Analytical kernel dossier Inference

These are source-level counts from the frozen configurations, not profiler measurements and not a latency model. The measured columns are bank-2 medians placed beside the predictions; the juxtaposition does not establish why any action is fast or slow.

Where each kernel comes from

  • old / tile (src/heliostune/kernel.py): Repository-authored conventional grouped tl.load/tl.dot kernel in the style of Triton's matrix-multiplication tutorial. The four larger new tile actions change launch parameters, not the algorithm. No novel GEMM algorithm claim.
  • persistent (src/heliostune/hopper_kernel.py): Tutorial-derived: Triton v3.4.0 09-persistent-matmul.py descriptor-persistent kernel, adapted and validated here; not an original persistent algorithm. Both expanded-bank configs disable warp specialisation and epilogue subtiling.
  • split_k (src/heliostune/action_expansion.py): Implemented in this repository: tensor-core partial tiles, unpadded FP32 workspace, separate Triton reduction, one FP16 narrowing. Split-K itself is an established technique, not a new algorithm. Distinct from the older non-tensor-core skinny_gemv.

Formulas

ideal bytes
2*(M*K+K*N+M*N) FP16 bytes, one read per operand and one output write
intensity
useful GEMM FLOPs 2*M*N*K divided by logical bytes; split-K adds workspace traffic to the denominator
launches
ordinary/persistent: 1; tensor-core split-K: 2 (partials and reduction)
masked rows
1-M/(Tm*BM); masked dot lanes, NOT a fraction of latency or HBM bytes
no cross tile reuse
2*(Tn*M*K+Tm*K*N+M*N) + workspace write/read; no inter-tile input reuse
padded dot
2*(Tm*BM)*(Tn*BN)*(S*Tk*BK); source-level padded dot work, not instruction counts
persistent balance
each persistent program strides tiles by SMs: at most ceil(Tm*Tn/P) tiles per program; balance = Tm*Tn/(P*ceil(Tm*Tn/P)), 1 when every program gets the same tile count
programs
ordinary: Tm*Tn; persistent: min(SMs,Tm*Tn); split-K: S*Tm*Tn plus ceil(M*N/256) reduction programs
tiles
Tm=ceil(M/BM), Tn=ceil(N/BN), Tk=ceil(ceil(K/BK)/S); S=1 except split-K
workspace
4*S*M*N FP32 bytes; write plus read = 8*S*M*N; unpadded; zero without split-K

What the dossier cannot say

  • No profiler counters, emitted instruction counts, occupancy model or predicted microseconds.
  • Persistent program count is not achieved occupancy; masked rows are not measured wasted time.
  • Logical traffic is not HBM traffic: caching/reloads, spills, descriptor scratch and allocation are unmodelled.
  • Pipeline stages and warp counts change scheduling/resources but not these source-level tensor byte counts.
  • A prediction beside a measurement is a juxtaposition, not a causal explanation of that measurement.

7 · Limits

  • One H100 collection, not a device-replication study. Repeated shapes are not independent devices.
  • Quantiles describe spread, not confidence intervals; individual timing samples were not retained. 5 of the 9 wins have margins below 1%.
  • Torch was timed first in each workload's order while candidate order was shuffled.
  • The baseline is allocating torch.matmul, not matched cuBLASLt or CUTLASS kernels or preallocated/graph endpoints.
  • Warmed medians omit compilation, acquisition and serving cost.
  • I did not rerun Thompson or transfer policies on the expanded bank: this is a better-actions result, not a better-autotuner result.
  • Bank 2 holds only finalists, so full-bank oracle regret and winner stability for the new bank are unidentifiable.
  • Exhaustive earlier-bank selection and in-sample oracles are descriptive; they are not a probe-budget or policy result.
  • Stored archives overlap (41 view pairs share identical cells); I never pool them as repetitions.
  • The kernel dossier is analytical. There is no profiler evidence for any cause of the remaining gap.
  • Ten new actions do not establish a hardware optimum or exhaust possible Triton kernels.
